1. About¶

I work on safe and trustworthy reinforcement learning for industrial process control. My research is organized around a single question: when can a control policy be trusted, if all we have is real closed-loop operating logs from a running plant?

Closed-loop logs are convenient but treacherous — the controller that generated them is itself part of the system, so action effects are often not identifiable from the data. I combine identifiability and causal diagnosis, physics-grounded digital twins, and uncertainty-calibrated safety layers to deploy learned policies only where the data supports them, and to abstain where it does not.

2. Research¶

2.1 Topics

Safe and offline reinforcement learning
Autonomous control of continuous industrial processes learned from logged operation, without exploratory interaction on the live plant.
Identifiability and causal diagnosis
Deciding when offline policy learning is reliable on closed-loop logs, and abstaining when action effects are not identifiable under controller-induced confounding.
Hybrid, physics-grounded digital twins
Sample-efficient and physically consistent surrogates for policy learning, validated on a twin built from 11 months of papermaking operation across 687 production lots.
Uncertainty quantification and conformal prediction
Coverage guarantees for surrogate models, including region-wise imbalance and Mondrian conformal prediction for heterogeneous operating regimes.
Tail-risk-aware, capacity-constrained control
Reducing rare but costly failures by constraining policy capacity, rather than by optimizing the mean return alone.
LLM- and RAG-based operator support
Retrieval-grounded assistants for real-time process monitoring and document lookup on the shop floor.

Keywords: Offline RL Safe RL Causal Identifiability Closed-Loop Confounding Digital Twin Conformal Prediction Tail-Risk Industrial Process Control
